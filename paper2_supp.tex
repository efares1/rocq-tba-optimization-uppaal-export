The Rocq preservation theorems hold for every TBA represented by the formula-indexed clock interface, independently of whether the automaton was generated by the MTL translator; measurements are limited to benchmark automata recorded in the project.

\section{Implementation and Reproduction Details}
\label{sec:supp-implementation}
The Rocq definitions and preservation proofs are in \texttt{proof/}; the optimizer and exporter functions are extracted into \texttt{tool/src/optim.ml}. The parser, Spot invocation, LBTT reader, fixed-point driver, and XML/DOT printers are hand-written. The importer, extraction cleanup script, OCaml runtime, external tools, and XML serialization remain outside the kernel-checked transformation theorems.

The upstream-generated measurements retain five runs per attempted configuration, each limited to 300 seconds. The current summary has 31 attempted configurations and 29 completed optimizer inputs. Machine details are in \path{evaluation/machine.txt}; raw runs and summary rows are in \path{evaluation/ours\_runs.tsv} and \path{evaluation/ours\_results.tsv}. The current R2 input, optimized graph, and export are in \path{evaluation/generated\_examples/R2/}, with command and provenance recorded in its README.

The Rocq transformations use existential initial clock values. UPPAAL starts clocks at zero, so deployment through the formula-indexed workflow relies on the proved \texttt{init\_free} condition. The lemma \path{init_free_accepts0} applies to every exported automaton on which the check succeeds; the check may fail on an external automaton whose acceptance relies on nonzero initial clock values.

\section{Pass-Level and Ablation Data}
The sixteen directly constructed records and larger parameterized families are defined in \path{evaluation/synthetic\_tba\_bench.ml}. These values inhabit the extracted \texttt{Optim.tBA} type; no third-party parser or external corpus is involved. \path{evaluation/synthetic\_tba\_passes.tsv} records the input, each operation in every changed round, the optimized or capped result, and explicit export. It records declared and graph-reachable locations separately, along with transitions, active clocks, reset occurrences, guard atoms, invariant counts, diagonal guards, elapsed time, and OCaml heap-capacity observations. \path{evaluation/synthetic\_tba\_memory.tsv} records peak resident set size from a separate process per small model. The corrected full trace and mechanism examples are preserved under \path{evaluation/runs/2026-10-10-synthetic-ablation-v6/}.

The leave-one-operation-out driver in \path{evaluation/synthetic\_tba\_ablation.tsv} repeats the remaining composite operations to stability for each variant. The complete variant is structurally compared with the extracted optimizer. This comparison validates the measurement harness's structural correspondence; it is neither an independent semantics proof nor a theorem for the altered pipelines. \path{evaluation/synthetic\_tba\_parameterized.tsv} gives intermediate sizes for duplicate-edge, unreachable-cascade, redundant-bound, synchronous-reset, and disjunctive-invariant families. The 50-round cap probe is preserved under \path{evaluation/runs/2026-10-10-round-cap-v4/}; size 51 changes after round 50, and size 64 continues changing after rounds 51 and 52. All these inputs are synthetic.

\section{Symbolic Transition Counts Before and After Optimization}
Table~\ref{tab:symbolic-before-after} gives, for every benchmark configuration that completes, the explicit
transitions of the optimizer input and of the optimized automaton, and the symbolic transitions of the
exported automaton with the optimizer disabled and enabled. Both symbolic counts apply the same grouping, so
their difference is attributable to the optimizer. The values are produced by
\path{evaluation/make_symbolic_before_after.py} from the raw output of \path{evaluation/run_symbolic_before_after.sh}. With the front end of the translation article (\texttt{-noweak -norecur}) and the optimizer disabled, the symbolic count equals the number of edges obtained by grouping the transitions of \texttt{compile}$(f)$ by source, target, guard, and resets, as counted in that article, on all 27 configurations that complete within 300 s (F1--F14, F3b, R1--R4, N1--N8); R5 does not finish in this setting and R6--R8 do not build. The check is \path{evaluation/run\_chain.sh}, with output in \path{evaluation/chain\_noweak.tsv}.

Per-instance statistics of the optimizer's reduction of reachable explicit transitions (optimizer input to optimized automaton), computed from the same table: over the 29 completed inputs, 20,204 to 1,500 transitions; without R6, 4,579 to 860 (81.2\%); without R5 and R6, 1,454 to 588 (59.6\%); on the 23 F and N inputs, 674 to 411 (39.0\%). \section{Random Formulas and Export Policies}
The 100 random formulas of the translation article (depth at most three, three propositions; \path{evaluation/random_formulas.tsv}) were run through the default pipeline with the optimizer enabled, by \path{evaluation/run_random_opt.sh}, with a limit of 120~s per formula; all 100 complete, the slowest in 1.2~s and none needs more than two optimization rounds. Over the 100 formulas, the explicit transitions of the optimizer input total 2,507 and those of the optimized automaton 1,509; locations 696 and 564; clocks 160 and 128. The optimizer reduces transitions on 57 formulas, locations on 25 and clocks on 26, and increases none of the three. The exported automaton has 566 locations and 1,547 transitions in total: the maximal-delay export adds difference guards on some formulas, and on two formulas it has one location more than the optimized automaton. The verified \texttt{init\_free} check succeeds on 95 formulas and fails on five (X35, X38, X65, X84, X100), for which the zero-initial-clock reading of UPPAAL is therefore not covered by the proved condition. These are size and timing measurements; they do not check language equivalence, which the proofs provide for the optimizer and the export.

Table~\ref{tab:export-policies} compares the two export policies on every configuration: the maximal-delay export (default) and the separately verified all-copy split (\texttt{-all-copy}), by \path{evaluation/run_export_policies.sh}. The two exports have the same number of invariants on every completed configuration. The all-copy export has exactly the transitions of the optimized automaton and no difference guards. The maximal-delay export has more transitions on F14 and R2--R6 (for example 1,837 against 640 on R6, with 3,360 difference guards) and the same number elsewhere; the totals over the 29 completed configurations are 3,209 and 1,500. R7 and R8 do not complete under either policy within 300~s. The comparison is of size; the choice between them for a given verification task is not evaluated here.

\section{Reproducing Synthetic Measurements}
From the repository root under WSL, the extracted-code measurements can be regenerated with:
\begingroup
\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
ocamlc -w -a -c tool/src/optim.ml \
  -o tmp/optim.cmo
ocamlc -w -a -I tmp \
  -o tmp/synthetic_tba_bench.exe \
  unix.cma tmp/optim.cmo \
  evaluation/synthetic_tba_bench.ml
tmp/synthetic_tba_bench.exe \
  > evaluation/synthetic_tba_passes.tsv
tmp/synthetic_tba_bench.exe --ablation \
  > evaluation/synthetic_tba_ablation.tsv
tmp/synthetic_tba_bench.exe --parameterized \
  > evaluation/synthetic_tba_parameterized.tsv
bash evaluation/run_synthetic_tba_memory.sh
python evaluation/make_synthetic_tba_table.py
python evaluation/make_synthetic_ablation_table.py
python evaluation/make_parameterized_summary.py
python evaluation/make_optimizer_aggregate_table.py
python evaluation/make_parameterized_trace_table.py
\end{verbatim}
\endgroup
The profiled full pass sequence is structurally compared with extracted \texttt{optimize\_step}; these runs measure the extracted implementation and do not compile Rocq proof source. The leave-one-operation-out variants are measurement harnesses, not separately proved optimizers.

\section{Archived Upstream Diagnostics}
Repository files retain formula-to-automaton timing, Spot representation, CASAAL, and cross-tool diagnostics under \path{evaluation/}. Those measurements concern upstream translation and its validation rather than the optimizer. They are excluded from this supplement to avoid attributing upstream preprocessing or backend behavior to the optimization contribution; they remain available as artifact data.

\section{UPPAAL Case-Study Artifacts}
The two system families, four configurations, UPPAAL queries, and verdicts are under \path{evaluation/case\_study/}. The eight queries were rerun with UPPAAL 5.0.0 \texttt{verifyta} (\path{uppaal\_results\_rerun\_2026-10-11.txt}) and agree with the archived verdicts (\path{uppaal\_results.txt}); the results contain no before/after runtime, memory, or zone counts. The matched TChecker artifacts are under \path{evaluation/runs/2026-10-13-tchecker-before-after/}. They report equal before/after verdicts and exploration on two response-system cases whose exported XML is byte-identical. TChecker timed-B\"uchi emptiness is not ordinary UPPAAL reachability, and the XML-to-TChecker adapter is not verified.
